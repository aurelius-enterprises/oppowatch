What the watch does

Daily USPTO filings.

Every new US trademark application in the USPTO daily file is checked against your marks for spelling and sound similarity in the same or coordinated classes.

Publications for opposition.

Marks published for opposition are checked the same way, so you see them while the window is still open.

Opposition-window deadlines.

Each published match shows the date the initial 30-day opposition window closes, plus a reminder date 10 days before it.

Facts, not verdicts.

Every row has the serial number and a TSDR link so you can check the record yourself. Similarity is shown as a match reason, never as a risk rating.

FAQ

Where does the data come from?

Public USPTO records: the daily trademark application file and marks published for opposition. Each match links to the USPTO's own TSDR record.

Is this legal advice?

No. The report shows records and dates only. It never recommends opposing, filing or doing nothing; for that, talk to a trademark attorney.

How is the opposition deadline worked out?

It is the initial 30-day window from the publication date. Extensions are possible, so check the USPTO TTAB page and the TSDR record for the actual status. How opposition deadlines work.

Which marks can I watch?

Any word mark, in the classes you choose. Matching covers spelling and sound similarity in the same or coordinated classes.

Does it cover other countries?

No. The watch covers US federal (USPTO) filings and publications only.

The fine print

This report lists public USPTO records that look similar to your watched mark, with the dates those records carry. It is information, not legal advice.
